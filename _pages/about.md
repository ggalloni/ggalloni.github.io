I work on the Cosmic Microwave Background and on primordial gravitational waves, at the
point where cosmological inference meets the statistics of data on the sphere. I build
**optimal (quadratic maximum-likelihood) power-spectrum estimators** and **exact
likelihoods** for large-scale CMB polarisation — the regime where the approximations that
serve the high-multipole sky break down, and where the signatures of inflation live. More
on the [research](/research/) page.

I am a member of the **LiteBIRD** and **Simons Observatory** collaborations, and I lead
their power-spectrum-estimation and likelihood efforts: within LiteBIRD I lead the
power-spectrum and likelihood programme and sit on the Interim Governance Board; within SO
I lead the LT1 subgroup of the Likelihood and Theory group and maintain SOLikeT. Almost
everything I do ends up as [open-source software](/software/).

Before Ferrara I took my PhD *cum laude* in Astronomy, Astrophysics and Space Science at
Sapienza University of Rome, with the research carried out at Rome Tor Vergata, under
Nicola Vittorio and Sabino Matarrese. I read Physics of the Universe at Padova, where my
master's work with Nicola Bartolo and Angelo Ricciardone started the primordial
gravitational-wave thread I am still pulling on. My bachelor's thesis, at the University of
Florida, was on adaptive thermal-lensing compensation for LIGO.

I teach Bayesian Statistics on the MSc in Physics at Ferrara, and I supervise or mentor
four researchers at three institutions in two countries.
